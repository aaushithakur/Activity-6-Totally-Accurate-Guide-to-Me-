02 / THE COLLABORATOR’S HANDBOOK

So, you’re
on my team.

A practical survival guide for working across my creative and technical worlds, inspired by the processes described in my portfolio.

Bring a clear goal,
an open mind, and useful evidence.

  1. Start with the intended impact.

    Define the audience and what the work should communicate. My portfolio connects creative decisions to emotional resonance and clear strategic goals.

  2. Bring both visual and sonic references.

    Think about colour, light, pacing, atmosphere, and rhythm together. My Photoshop, Premiere Pro, and music work all contribute to the experience.

  3. Leave room for craft and code.

    Choose the tool around the creative need. TunePy is my example of building a technical solution when existing audio tools impose limits.

  4. Give the data a solid foundation.

    For analytical work, make time for cleaning, preprocessing, and clear evaluation. These are parts of the conversational-AI workflow I describe at Sigma AI.

  5. Evaluate, share, and refine.

    Review the result against its purpose and bring the findings back to the team. My portfolio describes using analysis to improve AI systems and audience-facing work.

03 / MY GREATEST WEAKNESS

Big dreams.
Practical limits.

Even a big idea
needs a place to start.

!My greatest weakness:
dreaming bigger
than what’s practical.

Sometimes the dream outgrows the plan

I dream big, and sometimes my ideas go beyond what is practical. Seeing possibilities is part of how I create, but an idea still needs to fit what can realistically be done.

Give the dream a starting point

A useful reality check: what is the smallest achievable version of this idea? That question gives a big dream a practical first step.
